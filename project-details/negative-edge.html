Process

Sketching the Beginning of the Level

When I joined the Villains team, I was tasked with extending the existing level and creating more space for players to practise movement mechanics such as dashing, grappling, wall-running, and, of course, jumping. I drew a top-down map for the extension, grounding it in the team's existing worldbuilding. The layout included plenty of opportunities for backtracking and hidden collectibles. I deliberately left the area beyond the bridge in the top-left corner loosely defined so it could accommodate future changes.

Process

Testing the Blockout

After sketching the top-down map, I moved straight into the engine and used its modelling tools to shape the blockout. I tested the level's dimensions and spacing to ensure that jumps and dashes covered the intended distances.

Process

Reducing the Map to Meet the Team's Needs

Later in development, the team decided to cut some content, including a substantial portion of the level. Drawing on my backup plan, I revised the layout beyond the broken bridge to lead directly towards the next area, replacing the original, more complex route.

Process

Building the Environment

Once I had established the environment's layout with the blockout, I handed the set dressing over to another 3D artist. I provided guidelines, ongoing support, and feedback on models, textures, and shaders to help achieve the intended result.

Process

Building the Elevator

The elevator concept came to me while I was playing my favourite MMORPG, Final Fantasy XIV. It sparked an idea for linking the Canyon to the existing floating area. I gathered visual references and collaborated with the game designer to refine the concept. I then blocked out the elevator with primitive shapes using precise dimensions before handing its production over to the 3D art team.

Concept (1 / 3)
Process

Building the Boss Arena

While the art department handled set dressing for the Canyon and Elevator, I took responsibility for the boss arena. I reviewed the game designer's existing blockout and worked with them to adapt both the arena and the boss fight to the team's updated requirements. I developed the new visual direction in Maya, keeping the team involved throughout so I could incorporate feedback and adjust features as needed. Once the blockout was complete, I finished the area with textures, props, and interactive elements, creating the arena's current look, which received praise from all the playtesters and players we worked with.

Blockout (1 / 4)
Process

Building the Fortress

Once the boss arena was complete, I enclosed it within a fortress designed to serve as a major landmark. Its imposing presence was intended to build anticipation and signal that a boss fight was approaching. I positioned the fortress strategically so the entire structure could be framed from any point in the floating level, giving the environment a more dramatic composition.

Process

Enemy Placement and Balancing

After completing the environment, I collaborated with the design team to balance enemy placement, utility items, and health and shield stations. As the map layout evolved, we went through several rounds of iteration to find the right balance between fast-paced gameplay and difficulty.

Reflection

Final Thoughts

Negative Edge was a distinctive project to work on. I joined a project that was already in development and had to adapt to a unique art style that was far removed from my specialisation. I worked hard to match that visual direction while guiding the art department towards a cohesive result, ensuring that the new assets felt consistent with those already in the game.
